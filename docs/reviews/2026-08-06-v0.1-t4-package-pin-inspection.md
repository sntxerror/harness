# REVIEW — V0.1-T4 package pin inspection

Review date: 6 August 2026
Scope: verification of the three V0.1 package pins (`@gotgenes/pi-permission-system`, `pi-interactive-shell`, `@juicesharp/rpiv-ask-user-question`) against the blockers named in `tasks/V0.1-baseline-harness.md` T4 and in `HANDOFF.md`. Source was read from the published npm tarballs, extracted and inspected locally; **nothing was installed and nothing was executed** (`docs/01`, extension trust rule). This file records findings only — the decisions it forces are listed in §5 and are the operator's under ADR-036.

---

## 1. Provenance and supply-chain facts

All three packages exist, are MIT-licensed, publish TypeScript source rather than only bundles (so the behaviours below are read from the shipped code, not inferred), and **none declares a `preinstall`/`install`/`postinstall`/`prepare` script**. Installing with `--ignore-scripts` therefore costs nothing and remains the right default.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `@gotgenes/pi-permission-system` | **24.0.0** | 26 Jul 2026 | 170 since 3 May 2026 | `github.com/gotgenes/pi-packages` | `0698d8b6…cfcf0d32` |
| `pi-interactive-shell` | **0.14.0** | 29 Jul 2026 | 43 since 18 Jan 2026 | `github.com/nicobailon/pi-interactive-shell` | `9d4473e2…1e4f508a54` |
| `@juicesharp/rpiv-ask-user-question` | **2.4.0** | 3 Aug 2026 | 111 since 13 Apr 2026 | `github.com/juicesharp/rpiv-mono` | `9dc02c44…d614e52d2b` |

Two supply-chain facts carry forward. First, `pi-interactive-shell` depends on **`zigpty`** — a native PTY binding built with Zig, shipping prebuilt binaries with no install script. It is the only native code in the set and the only component whose behaviour cannot be established by reading TypeScript. Second, `@juicesharp/rpiv-ask-user-question` drags in **`@juicesharp/rpiv-config`** (dependency) and **`@juicesharp/rpiv-i18n`** (peer) from the same author, so accepting this pin accepts three packages, not one, and those two have not been inspected here.

Peer-dependency compatibility against the installed Pi **0.83.0** is asserted only by the permission system, which requires `@earendil-works/pi-coding-agent >=0.79.0`. The other two declare `*` — no compatibility guarantee, so both must be verified empirically at T3/T5/T6 rather than assumed from the manifest.

The release cadence of the permission system deserves its own note: **24 majors in under three months**, the most recent five days before this review. That is not a reason to reject it — the churn is visible, changelogged and semver-honest — but it does mean the pin will go stale fast and that R-050 (upgrading a pin is a task with inspection notes, not a routine bump) will bite here more than anywhere else in the system.

## 2. `@gotgenes/pi-permission-system` — the R-001 blocker

**Verdict: the pin is sound, but upstream cannot give us the invariant. The fallback named in `HANDOFF.md` is required, not optional.**

Our invariant is parse-once at `session_start`, hashed into the manifest, with config edits mid-session having no effect. The good news is that the failure mode we most feared is absent: there is **no filesystem watcher anywhere in the package** — no `fs.watch`, no `watchFile`, no `chokidar`. Editing `config.json` on disk during a live session does nothing on its own. That is the property that makes a guard tractable at all.

The bad news is that two *explicit* mid-session mutation paths exist, both event-driven rather than ambient:

| Path | Mechanism | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pi `/reload` | `resources_discover` with `reason === "reload"` → `SessionLifecycleHandler.handleResourcesDiscover` → `PermissionSession.reload()` → `permissionManager.configureForCwd()` (`src/handlers/lifecycle.ts:84`, `src/permission-session.ts:132`) | Re-reads merged policy from disk and clears skill entries. `session_start` with `reason: "reload"` additionally calls `refreshConfig()` + `resetForNewSession()` |
| `/permission-system` command | `src/config-modal.ts` → `ConfigStore.save()` → atomic `writeFileSync` + `renameSync` to the global config path (`src/config-store.ts:145`) | Writes and applies four runtime knobs live, **including `yoloMode`** |

`yoloMode` is the sharp edge. Its own description is "auto-approve ask-state permission checks, including subagent approval forwarding" — a single mid-session toggle that disables the ask path wholesale, persisted to global config, reachable from a slash command. Any freeze design that covers policy rules but leaves the extension-config modal reachable has not frozen anything that matters.

Two things are *correctly* mutable and should not be confused with the above. Session approvals ("allow for this session") live in `src/session-rules.ts`, are held in memory, are never persisted, and are cleared at `session_shutdown` — that is the intended escalation channel and is compatible with our invariant. And project-trust gating (#644) deliberately withholds project scope until trust is granted, failing closed at construction (`src/index.ts:175-176`), which is behaviour we want.

**Consequence.** A launcher guard is feasible and is the cheaper of the two fallbacks: the mutation surface is small, explicit and event-shaped, so a guard that (a) does not register the `resources_discover` reload path, (b) does not register the `/permission-system` command, and (c) hashes the resolved config at `session_start` into the manifest, closes both paths without patching upstream logic. A pinned patch is the alternative if upstream's registration is not interceptable from a wrapper. Which of the two we adopt is a decision, not a finding — see §5.

**Integration bonus.** The package documents a cross-extension service API (`docs/cross-extension-api.md`) with `checkPermission`, `getToolPermission`, `registerToolInputFormatter` and `registerToolAccessExtractor`, and states its stability guarantee in semver terms. It also documents reload safety explicitly and advises callers to resolve the service per use rather than caching the reference. This is the surface through which the PTY gap in §3 is closed.

## 3. `pi-interactive-shell` — PTY, secrets, and the permission alias

**Verdict: accept the pin, but neither guarantee in R-005/R-006 is provided by this package. Both must be imposed by us.**

### 3.1 Secret entry

The package contains **zero occurrences** of `password`, `secret`, `redact`, `mask`, `sensitive`, `credential` or `sudo` across its entire source and documentation. There is no redaction layer, no secret-aware prompt detection, and no notion of protected input.

Reading the capture path explains what that does and does not mean. `PtySession` accumulates a `rawOutput` string exclusively from `ptyProcess.onData` — the child's **output** stream (`pty-session.ts:212-250`). The input path, `write(data)` at `pty-session.ts:336`, forwards keystrokes straight to the PTY and **does not append them to `rawOutput`**. So for `sudo` specifically the requirement is satisfied, but by inheritance rather than by design: `sudo` disables terminal echo, the typed password never appears on the output stream, and therefore never enters the buffer, the headless xterm screen, or the tool result.

That guarantee is only as good as the child program's terminal discipline. Anything that echoes what it reads — a shell script using a bare `read`, a CLI prompting for an API key inline, a paste into a non-masking field — puts the secret into `rawOutput`, from there into the model-visible tool result, and from there into a file on disk: `handoff-utils.ts:90` writes a plaintext transcript tail to `~/.pi/cache/interactive-shell/snapshot-<timestamp>-pid<n>.log` whenever `handoffSnapshotEnabled` is set. `rawOutput` itself is a 1 MiB rolling buffer (`pty-log.ts`), and the tool exposes it through incremental and paginated readers.

So the operative controls are: keep `handoffSnapshotEnabled` **off** in our profile (it is config-gated, so this is a profile setting rather than a patch), continue to require that privileged commands are the operator's to run (`CLAUDE.md`), and treat "password typed directly, never captured" as a property of the *prompting program*, which V0.1-T7's secret-entry redaction probe must test with a deliberately echoing prompt rather than only with `sudo`.

### 3.2 Whether any path bypasses the permission alias

> **Correction (7 August 2026).** The conclusion originally recorded in this section — that `interactive_shell` escapes command-level gating and that `registerToolAccessExtractor` is the only available hook — was **wrong**, because it searched the shell package for permission integration rather than the permission package for a shell-tool hook. The permission system provides `shellTools`, a first-class config mapping present in 24.0.0 whose schema states that recording an alias gates the tool "through the same bash enforcement stack as native `bash` (command decomposition, wrapper flooring, path/external-directory token gates, and `bash:` rules)" (`src/config-schema.ts:129`, `src/access-intent/tool-kind.ts:56`). `docs/01` §2.1 already specified the exact entry. The gating half therefore needs configuration, not code. What survives the correction is recorded below and in ADR-037: `mode` is still model-supplied with no allowlist, and a call carrying only `sessionId` and `input` still presents an **empty** command to the gate, matching `"*": "allow"` — so per-call gating cannot see keystroke injection into a live session, and sticky per-session classification is still required (R-091).

Yes — by omission. The package registers a single model-facing tool, **`interactive_shell`** (`tool-schema.ts:3`), whose parameters include a free-form `command` string executed in a PTY, plus `input`/`inputKeys`/`inputHex`/`inputPaste` for injecting keystrokes into a live session. There is **no permission-system integration anywhere in the package** — no import, no service lookup, no gate call.

Whether that constitutes a bypass depends on the permission system's generic tool gate, and the answer from §2's documentation is precise: path gating is automatic for tools exposing `input.path`, MCP calls are read from `input.arguments.path`, and **"`bash` is never extracted here — it has its own token-based path gates."** `interactive_shell` matches none of those. Its command string is not the `bash` tool, so it does not receive the tree-sitter-bash command-level parsing, and it carries no `path` key, so it does not receive automatic path extraction. It would be evaluated as an opaque tool call under a whole-tool rule — allow or ask for *everything the tool can do* — which is exactly the granularity collapse the permission design exists to prevent.

`registerToolAccessExtractor("interactive_shell", …)` is the documented hook, but it is scoped to extracting **a single filesystem path**, not to parsing a command line. It is therefore a partial remedy, not a complete one. Closing this properly means routing `interactive_shell`'s `command` through the same access-intent extraction the `bash` tool receives, which is a change to how we wire the two extensions together and is a decision (§5).

Two further surfaces are user-facing rather than model-facing and so are outside the tool gate by construction: the `/spawn`, `/attach` and `/dismiss` commands, where `/spawn` launches executables from `config.spawn.commands` in a git worktree. They are the operator's to invoke, but they are worth knowing about before we assume the tool schema is the whole attack surface.

## 4. `@juicesharp/rpiv-ask-user-question` — schema fit

**Verdict: accept the pin; an adapter is required, and the mapping is lossy in one direction and under-specified in the other.**

The upstream contract (`tool/types.ts`, typebox) is a **batch questionnaire**: 1–4 questions per call, each with a `question` string, a `header` capped at 16 characters, 2–4 `options` where each option is an object `{label ≤60 chars, description, preview?}`, and an optional `multiSelect`. Answers come back as `{questionIndex, question, kind: "option"|"custom"|"multi", answer, selected?, notes?, preview?}`. Three labels are reserved and rejected at runtime: `"Other"` plus two sentinel row labels.

Our `schemas/UserQuestionV1.schema.json` is a **single-question record with a lifecycle**. The divergences:

| Concern | Upstream | `UserQuestionV1` | Adapter obligation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cardinality | batch of 1–4 | one question per record | Fan out N questions into N records sharing a `runId`; derive `questionId` from `questionIndex` |
| Choices | objects with `description`/`preview` | `choices: string[]` | Map `options[].label` → `choices[]`; `description` and `preview` have **no home in our schema** and are dropped |
| Constraints | hard-enforced (2–4 options, ≤16-char header, ≤60-char label, reserved labels rejected) | unconstrained | Ours is the more permissive schema, so it can carry theirs; the reverse can fail validation. Filter reserved labels before dispatch |
| `sensitivity` | **absent** | required enum | Assigned by us, never sourced from the tool |
| `deadline`, `operationHash`, `status` | absent (synchronous call/response) | required lifecycle | Adapter owns the transitions; `expired` and `cancelled` have no upstream representation |
| `answeredBy` | absent (single local user) | field present | Filled from session identity |

The `sensitivity` gap is the one with teeth, and it connects back to §3.1. This tool is a **questionnaire, not a credential-entry surface**: its free-text row echoes typed characters, and answers are formatted into an LLM-facing envelope (`tool/format-answer.ts`, `tool/response-envelope.ts`). A question classified `credential` must therefore never be routed through it. Because upstream has no sensitivity concept of its own, that rule can only live in our adapter — which is an argument for the adapter existing regardless of the schema mismatch, rather than us relaxing `UserQuestionV1` to match upstream's shape.

One further path deserves a look before T6 and was not fully traced here: `state/external-editor.ts` spawns `$EDITOR` over a temporary file, which is another way answer text reaches disk.

## 5. Decisions this forces (ADR-036 — operator's, not taken here)

1. **R-001 fallback mechanism** — launcher guard versus pinned patch, given that §2 shows the mutation surface is two explicit event/command registrations rather than a watcher. Affects `docs/02` and V0.1-T5.
2. **Whether `interactive_shell` is admitted at all before its command string is gated at command level**, and if so under what interim rule. Affects V0.1-T6 and the R-003 probes in T7.
3. **Adapter placement and ownership for `UserQuestionV1`** — one owner per responsibility (`docs/01` Outcome table) — plus whether `description`/`preview` justify extending the schema or are deliberately discarded.
4. **Accepting `@juicesharp/rpiv-config` and `@juicesharp/rpiv-i18n`** as uninspected transitive pins, or inspecting them first.
5. **Pin-refresh policy for the permission system** given 24 majors in three months.

New risk material implied by §2 and §3 (the `yoloMode` mid-session toggle; `interactive_shell` escaping command-level gating; the handoff snapshot writing un-redacted terminal tails to disk) belongs in `docs/03` with mitigations before any of it reaches config, per the change discipline in `CLAUDE.md`. It is not added here, because a review records findings and changes nothing.
